# Commercial licence

Tricot's core and its command line (the `tricot` command) are open source under the [GNU Affero General Public License, version 3](LICENSE) (AGPL-3.0-only). They are free for everyone, companies of any size included. You don't need anything from us to use Tricot, to run it for your team, or to change it. The source is at https://github.com/TricotStudio/tricot.

## What the AGPL asks in return

If you pass Tricot on to others, changed or not, they get its source under the same licence. If you change Tricot and let people use your changed version over a network, you offer them the source of your changes. That's all: the AGPL covers Tricot's code, never your boards or what you plan with them.

## When you need a commercial licence

A commercial licence from Tricot Studio is for anyone who can't meet those terms, for example if you:

- **embed Tricot in a closed-source product**, or ship it inside one, and don't want to release that product under the AGPL;
- **offer a changed Tricot over a network**, as a hosted or managed service for instance, without sharing your changes;
- **work for a company whose policy doesn't allow AGPL software**.

It gives you the same code under terms without those obligations. The text in [LICENSE](LICENSE) is what counts; this page explains it. Not sure which side you're on? Ask.

## Examples

| Situation | Licence |
| --- | --- |
| You plan your own projects with agents on your laptop | AGPL, free |
| A 40-person agency uses it for all its client projects | AGPL, free |
| A university course has students use it for their coursework | AGPL, free |
| Someone changes Tricot and publishes the changes under the AGPL | AGPL, free |
| A company ships Tricot inside its own closed-source product | Commercial |
| A company runs a changed Tricot as a service for its customers and keeps the changes to itself | Commercial |
| A company's policy rules out AGPL software, but it wants its teams on Tricot | Commercial |

## The desktop app and team features

Tricot Studio's desktop app and its team features (shared boards, sign-in, roles and sync) are separate products. They are closed source, under their own terms, and neither the AGPL nor this page covers them.

## The name

"Tricot" is Tricot Studio's name. The AGPL lets you fork the code, but it doesn't grant the name: a fork, or a service built on one, needs a name of its own.
