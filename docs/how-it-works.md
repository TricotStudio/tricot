# How Tricot works

Back to the [README](../README.md).

## Working with agents

Every change is logged with who made it and when. The log doubles as the sync cursor agents poll with.

**Pointing a message at one agent.** In a pin's comment, a thread message or the chat bar, **@** lists the board's agents, then its people, and Enter writes the name; the message reads as asked of that agent. An agent that can do one message on another model (Claude Code, through a subagent) shows its model and thinking level on its row, faint at its rule (the model and thinking level it starts on, set in its panel): **→** moves into them, **↑ ↓** change each, **←** comes back out, and Enter writes a tag, `@Claude:opus:high`, which is still a mention of Claude, marked as one. That message's work is done on that model and thinking level; Claude's own session stays as it is. The other agents' rows say *its own model*. The same tag works typed by hand, and in the terminal ([docs/cli.md](../cli.md#the-model-and-thinking-level-an-agent-starts-on)).

**A lens for one message.** Every thread looks at the project through a lens (General, Legal, Design…) that says how agents answer; the chip under its title changes it. In the chat bar, **^** at the start of a word lists the lenses, with each one's brief and tint and a tick on the thread's own, and Enter writes `^Design` as a chip, like an @ mention; a name is read as in an agent's terminal, whatever its case. It is a command, not content: what is sent leaves it out, and the message carries that lens, shown as a small `^Design` chip by its author's name. The agents it is for answer that message through it, in place of the thread's lens (their updates say *For this message, answer through its Design lens instead of the thread's Legal lens*), and the next message goes back to the thread's. The thread's own lens is never changed by it, and `^General` asks for none. A message of a new thread can ask for one too. Typed at the start of a message straight into Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Hermes Agent, Pi or OMP, `^Design` asks the same, through Tricot's prompt hook or, in Pi and OMP, its steps extension ([docs/cli.md](../cli.md#live-steps)); an agent that gets it as plain text finds the lenses in its guide.

**A coordinator and the threads it hands out.** Each agent you start with `tricot claude` (or `tricot claude codex`, one for each) is a coordinator of the board, and answers the chat bar: a thread you start there is the one you @-mention, else the one that made the page or was last asked about the file you are on, if it is one, else the one active last, unless you @-mention an agent that isn't one. It answers a quick question in place and does no work itself; anything that takes work it sends on, to the thread that work belongs to, whose owner hears it at once (a follow-up of a thread, a question about it included, goes there too: what you asked moves into that thread, and the one the bar started for it goes, so your conversation stays one thread), or to a new thread, whose owner, of the coordinator's own kind, Tricot starts for it in a tab of its own in the coordinator's terminal (*Claude · Sign-in flow*, at most three at a time for each coordinator, or as many as its agent's panel says). That owner plans the work, runs its own subagents as scouts and workers, whose steps show live in the thread, and reports and asks there. You follow it: the conversation moves to the thread your message went to, with a way back, the answer peeking over the chat bar says where it went and opens it, or the chat bar offers to follow it if you were elsewhere, and a row of pills over the chat bar lists the threads at work, those waiting on you first. Your reply in one sets it back to work and wakes its owner, and an owner that is gone, as after Tricot restarted, is started again for it ahead of other work, on the session it had where its agent can (Claude Code can), so it picks up with all it knew; when it is done, the coordinator says so where you asked. Work finished that waits on your OK before it is committed is *in review*: you answer *Looks good, commit it* or *Needs changes* with a note, which goes to its owner as your message and sets it back to work, while a plain reply leaves it waiting, though its owner still reads it; after your OK its owner commits it and only then marks it done, so done always means landed. The coordinator, the orchestrators, the scouts and the workers each take the model and thinking level set for them in the agent's panel (`src/core/roles.ts`). An agent you start yourself and tell to listen on a board (by `tricot listen`) is a coordinator of it too, and is told so, so the board works the same however its agents were connected: with no terminal to start owners in, it owns the threads it sends work to itself and hands each one's work to a subagent of its own in the background, whose steps, plan and files show in that thread and on its pill. It stays a coordinator, with the tools for it, while it is connected, when its client connects again too, and through a restart of Tricot when it is back within a few minutes; after longer, it is one again, and told so, as it next listens. Pairing on a piece (*Pair* in a block's full view, or on a file in Files) offers a session of its own first, where a terminal holding the coordinator can start one: *New Claude session*, one for the whole page (a Sketchbook page, Files, or the board context), which your first request on any piece there starts in a tab of the coordinator's terminal as *Claude · Pair Mood board* (*Claude · Pair Files* in Files), owning a thread of its own about the page (*Pair Mood board*: one strand on Home's yarn and one row in Work), while the full view says it is starting, or why the terminal holds it back (it runs three pairing sessions at once, apart from the owners of work, and holds nine tabs). It acts on a small request itself, right away, and hands a big one (several files, a long build or test run), or one on another piece while it is still on one, to a background agent of its own, so nothing queues; every request and reply passes through it, it replies on each request's pin once its work is done, and its steps, its workers' too, show on the pins waiting on it; you can still pick an agent on the board, the coordinator last, which pairing never takes unless you pick it, so it stays free for the chat bar. Every piece you pair on there with that kind goes to it, and moving from one to the next goes on in it. When you leave the page's pairing (every full view there closed for a few minutes, idle for half an hour, or its page closed), it is told to set its thread done with what changed, which is its one *Done* line on Home, and Tricot sets it done with the pairing's summary when it doesn't within a few minutes. Pairing on that page again is the same session, its thread kept; another person gets one of their own, and so does another agent kind. Work never starts in a quick question: of each one that comes to it, a coordinator is told to answer a question there and send anything that is work on before anyone starts on it, and an agent can't mark a quick question as at work, so every piece of work has a thread of its own, a strand on Home's yarn and a row in the threads list.

**Quick questions stay in the conversation.** What you ask from the chat bar, wherever you are, is a quick question of the board's conversation, read whole on its Home. Each ask is still a thread of its own as stored, and only work handed out has a status, which is what tells the two apart, so nothing stored changed and an older Tricot reads the board as before. The threads list (the chat bar's threads button, Home's ball of yarn, the terminal's threads panel) shows the conversation as one row, *Conversation with Claude*, which opens it on Home, then the work, a row each; the count on the button and the ball is the work waiting on you, as the conversation marks what is new in it itself. Asked from the floating chat bar (over a Sketchbook page, a file in Files, any view but Home), the answer peeks over the bar as it comes, in a card that says the agent is on it until it answers, where the ask went if it was sent on as work, and that it was taken on if it was, with *Open on Home* and ✕ (or Esc); the dock opens no thread for it, and your next ask takes its place. The bell says what agents answered you there as one row, *Claude answered you*, which opens Home's conversation at the answer, and search finds what was said in every thread: a message in the conversation opens there, one in work opens its thread. Agents read every thread as before.

**How a conversation reads.** In every conversation, Home's, a thread opened there and the thread panel's, your own messages are compact bubbles on the right and agents' replies read as prose on the left, a step larger than before, with lines that stop at about 70 characters and the first paragraph of a reply a little heavier, so you can skim the leads. Another person's message reads on the left under their name, as an agent's does. Messages one author sends in a row sit close together, and turns sit apart. Comments under a block or on a card read as before.

**What a message made.** The blocks an agent made answering show under its reply as small pictures of themselves, not as lines: a picture or a screenshot as a thumbnail, a note's first lines, a table's first rows, a diagram or a chart drawn small, code from the lines it highlights, a diff's totals and files, a link's title and site. As many sit side by side as the conversation has room for (three on Home, two in the thread panel, one on a phone), with the page they are on under them and *+N more*, which shows the rest. A picture is not the canvas: nothing in it can be edited, and it is drawn as it comes into view, so a long conversation stays quick. A click opens the block on its page, as before. A block of a kind with no picture (a 3D scene, a website, a shape) reads as a line, and so does a comment.

**A board's Home.** The *Home* tab is the board's conversation, with the work it sent on beside it. Its main pane gathers what you asked from the chat bar and what the coordinator answered, oldest first, with the day and where you were last here between them; an ask made over a page or a file says so beside it (*Asked on Checkout flow*), which opens that page or file, and *Open thread* on a message whose thread is work of its own opens it there. Under an ask that was sent on, or whose own thread an agent took on, a card names the thread and where its work stands. Beside the conversation runs its yarn: the conversation is one strand, plied from the two yarns of Tricot's mark, with a stitch at each message, and each thread an agent owns knits off it where it was sent on, in the colour of where it stands, from the board's one map of where work stands: blue at work (in progress), violet when it needs you (in review), green when done, and stone, dotted, while its agent hasn't started (to do), and is tied off with a knot when it is done. Time runs down the yarn quietly, in your own clock, in small words at the left of the conversation's strand: the hour, by the first message said in it, once where the hour turns (a day's first too, under its divider; where messages are hours apart, only the later hour; minutes apart, none), and each thread's own times: when it started, where it knits off, and when its work was done, beside its knot (*9:01 PM*), in the day it was done. They scroll with the yarn, under the ball. *Work*, on the right, lists that work by where it stands: *Needs you*, *In review*, *In progress* (with what its agent is doing now and how far along its plan is), *To do* and *Done*. Pointing at a row lights its strand and its card. Home is made of cloth: the board's ground is knitted, the conversation lies on a sheet of paper over it with the chat bar at its foot and its yarn running down the sheet's left edge, the times just outside on the cloth, and a day, or where you were last here, is a band of stitches across it. Work is docked at the right, the whole height of Home, and the sheet is centred in the room Work leaves, so Work never covers the conversation, which moves over as Work folds or unfolds. The button at Work's top right folds it into a slim rail, which keeps a count of what waits on you, and the rail unfolds it again; this browser remembers which you chose, and until you choose, Work starts open on a window wide enough for it beside the column and folded on a narrower one. Esc inside Work also folds it for now, while your choice stays as it was; docked, it covers nothing, so a click elsewhere leaves it open. Pointing at a strand names its thread and when its work ran, with its day if not today (*done · 8:40 – 9:01 PM (21m)*, *needs you · started 8:40 PM*), and clicking it opens the thread in the main pane, as a card under an ask or a row in Work does, at an address of its own, with its agent's plan under its row. There its title heads the pane, and the bar at the foot answers in it, with *Replying in* and the thread's name over it; while its work waits for your review, the same two answers sit over the bar with the totals of what changed (*4 files, +43 −7, and 3 blocks*), and a plain reply from the bar leaves it waiting; *Conversation*, a click on the conversation's strand beside the thread (pointing at it says *Back to the conversation*) or Esc goes back, keeping where you had scrolled, and the bar asks something new, as the chat bar does. Tab reaches the yarn: ← and → move between strands, Enter opens one, and Esc leaves the yarn. At the top of the yarn sits a ball of yarn, which stays there as the conversation scrolls: every strand that runs on past the top unwinds from it, the conversation's own included, it carries the count of work waiting on you, and clicking it opens the threads list, the conversation and then the work, each with its strand's square. The same ball, drawn small, marks threads everywhere else, the chat bar's threads button included. The floating chat bar and the thread panel stay off Home, which has its own: what opens a thread elsewhere (the bell, a notification) opens it in Home's main pane while you are there. When work an agent owns needs you, the conversation relays it, so you can stay there: an agent's question to you, work waiting for your review with its summary, which folds to your verdict once you gave it, a thread's work done with its summary, and, where a part adds cards, one that is blocked. Between those, a thread's latest word comes back too, when its agent said it, though it doesn't wait on you: one card for the thread, three lines of it, which moves down as the agent says more and says how many of its words came before it (*2 earlier in the thread*), and gives way to a question, a review or done, so done is said once. A pairing comes back the same way: the latest word of a pairing session of your own on a page (a Sketchbook page, Files, or the board context), one card for each part you asked about on any of its pieces, led by the piece and where it is (*Mood board · palette.css*, *timeline.css in Files*), in the colour of where its session stands and all stitched to the session's one strand; *Reply* sends your message to that agent on its pin, as pairing does, and *Open* opens the piece's full view at that part, paired with it again. A pairing with an agent already on the board stays on its pins, and another person's stays theirs. Each comes as a card led by its thread's name, which opens the thread, edged in the colour of where the thread stands and with its strand stitched in. *Reply* on a question or a thread's latest word aims the bar at its thread for that one message, and once it is answered, from the conversation or in the thread, the card folds to a line saying what was answered. A review shows the previews of what changed under its summary, with *Looks good, commit it* and *Needs changes*, which opens a note in the card (Esc cancels it); once you gave your verdict, it folds to a line saying it (*You approved it*). What came in while you were away arrives as one card under *Since you were last here*, what needs you first, then the threads' latest words and the pairings' (*1 thread and 2 pairings moved while you were away*). It shows what threads already hold, and stores nothing of its own. Boards open on Home. The chat in the Tricot tab of [`tricot claude`](cli.md#several-agents-in-one-terminal) is the same conversation, the yarn a narrow column of lanes at its left.

**Reactions.** Under any comment or thread message, the smiley button adds 👍, 👎 or 👀; click your own again to take it back. The agent that wrote it reads the reaction in its updates. Agents put 👀 on your comment the moment it reaches them, so you know it was seen; that is quiet, waking no other agent. Agents react too (`react`), when a reply would only say yes, no or seen.

## Who's here

*Live* in the header says which agents have used the board in the last five minutes and what each is doing: *listening* (waiting on the board, so it sees you right away), *asleep* (its turn is over, and Tricot wakes it), *working* (busy between checks) or *not checking* (quiet for two minutes or more). A listening agent wakes only for what concerns it: a message, mention or request for it or for any agent, a person pairing with it, or a reply from whoever it @-mentioned. Other agents' progress and canvas edits don't wake it; it reads them when it next wakes. An agent Tricot starts in its terminal (`tricot claude`, `tricot codex`) doesn't wait on the board at all, as each wait costs it a whole model turn: it ends its turn when it has nothing to do, and shows as *asleep*. When something concerns it, Tricot types a line in its prompt ("Something on the board needs you: read your updates."), once its turn is over and you aren't typing in its tab, and checks that a turn began; a wake that didn't start one shows as stuck, on the board and in the terminal. Its subagents' calls neither wake it nor read for it: what concerns it stays due until it ends a turn having read it, or is woken for it. An agent you connect on your own sleeps too when its client starts a turn of its own as a command it runs in the background ends (Claude Code 2.1.288 or later, on a board that listens on this computer alone): its wait gives it a `tricot listen` command to run that way before it ends its turn, the command ends when something concerns it, and its end starts the agent's next turn. A wake that started no turn within three minutes shows as stuck until the agent calls again. That, or a command stopped for good, is a sleep gone wrong, and once is no reason to stop: the computer may have slept through the wake, or the agent been busy, so its next wait gives it the command again, as it does after a wait its client cut off. Only after two in a row, with no wake answered between, does it wait on the board for half an hour, as every other client does, before it is given the command again. Agents can't be pushed to, so every tool call also tells them about activity they haven't read yet. An agent that can run background subagents is told to orchestrate: it hands each piece of work to a subagent and stays on the board, listening, or asleep for Tricot to wake.

**A Codex you connect yourself.** Codex 0.166 or later, connected with `tricot connect codex`, sleeps and wakes the same way, through Codex itself: each of its calls says which Codex session it comes from, so its waits end its turn and it shows as *asleep* (as it does once its session has been quiet for 20 seconds), and when something concerns it, Tricot runs `codex queue` to hand that session one fixed line, to read its updates. Codex takes it as a turn at once, or once the turn it is in is over, never cutting one short. A wake its session doesn't answer within three minutes shows as stuck, and its waits wait from then on. Only a Tricot on the computer Codex runs on wakes it.

**Since you were away.** Leaving a board (switching tabs, closing it, going back to the list) records what you'd seen. When you return, what others changed since is marked: a dot on the Sketchbook tab and on each changed page, and a *new* badge on changed canvas blocks. Selecting a block marks it seen.

## Project folder and files

**Project folder.** A board can name its project folder: choose or change it in the board’s Files view (the folder icon before the tabs, there on every board: *Choose folder…* on a board without one, which opens the system's folder dialog and sets the folder picked (where the computer has none, a field to type its path); *Change folder…* in the menu of the folder named beside the view's title on one with it, where *Read the folder again* is too, though the view follows the folder live; an archived board keeps its folder), or set it with the agent tool `set_project_folder` (`null` clears it). The folder must exist inside the home directory, outside home-level hidden folders and `.git`. First run records the project folder when Tricot was started inside a project under home. Older boards keep no folder until one is set.

**Files.** A board with a project folder has a *Files* view, behind the folder icon before the tabs: the folder's files at the left, to search and filter, and the file you pick beside them, to *Read* (a Markdown, Mermaid, CSV or picture file opens drawn as the board draws it, its *Source* a switch away), to *Edit* in place, saved over the file only over the version you opened, or to *Pair* on with an agent, which opens the file's block on the board's Files canvas, where you point at lines and ask. The Files canvas is a canvas of the board's own, apart from the Sketchbook, with a code block for each file someone edited, paired on or asked about; agents read it with `get_canvas(page_id)`, and `get_board` names it. The chat bar over Files asks about the file you have open: *Ask Claude about src/pay.ts…*, or *Ask agents about src/pay.ts…* when no agent has been asked about it. What you send starts a thread on the file's block, a quick question whose answer peeks over the bar, as one asked over a Sketchbook page is. It goes through the General lens unless the message opens with `^Lens`, and to the agent you last asked about the file (the latest comment on its lines that an agent wrote or that @-mentions one, which is what pairing leaves), unless you @-mention another; where none was asked, the first agent to reply answers. The file's block is made with the thread if the Files canvas has none yet, and is brought up to the file as it is now if it has, so a thread that can't start leaves no block behind. The thread is tagged *Files · src/pay.ts* under its title. The agent talks the file through with you in words first, reads the file itself in its repository (the block is a copy from when the file was last opened here), and changes the file only once you ask for a change or agree to what it suggests. With no file open (the list, nothing picked, or a picture, which has no text to ask about) the bar is the open-ended one, *What do you want to work through?*, and starts a general thread. Selecting lines is Pair's job: its pins already point at them.

## Canvases

A board's **Sketchbook** tab holds **pages**: a canvas each, with every kind of block, for an idea you are exploring with agents. A new board has none; *New page* makes one. The page shown fills the tab, and its **Pages** button, at the canvas's top left before *Add*, names it. Pages opens a menu of every page in order, the one shown checked and a dot on each with changes you haven't seen (the button has a dot too when one does): pick one to show it, and drag one, or press Alt (Option) and an arrow on it, to move it among the others. Under the pages, *Rename page* (or F2 on the button) turns the button's name into a field: Enter keeps the new name, Escape puts the old one back. *Delete page* deletes the page shown, after asking, as it takes the page's blocks with it. *New page* at the menu's foot makes a page and opens it, its name ready to type. A read-only board's menu only lists its pages. The pages, their names and their order are everyone's; the page you showed last is remembered in this browser. Agents read and work on a page as on any canvas, with `page_id` (`get_board` lists the pages), and make and rename pages (`add_page`, `update_page`); only people arrange and delete them. A page's pins keep their own comment threads. **Show me on Tricot**: in an agent's own terminal (Claude Code, Codex, the others), ask it in plain words to see something on or in Tricot, the board or the canvas, "show me how signup works on Tricot" or "show me the last dev log in tricot", and it makes a page showing it and answers with the page's link; it never moves your browser. With [live steps](../cli.md#live-steps) on, the agent's prompt hook spots such a request and adds a line beside it saying to make the page, or, when Tricot isn't running, that it isn't and to start it, while a message that only mentions Tricot, or asks about code in a project named tricot, goes as typed. It uses the board it works with, else the one that fits its folder, and asks you which when it can't tell, or before it makes a new one. A page an agent made says so in a quiet line under *Pages*: *Asked in Claude Code: “show me how signup works”*, or *Made by Codex* when it wasn't asked, and that the agent answers here. What you ask on such a page goes to that agent unless you @-mention another: the chat bar over it (*Ask Claude about this page…*) starts a thread about the page, routed to it as a lens can be, and so is what you ask about blocks you select. The chat bar over any page starts a thread about that page, which agents answer there. An agent that makes a page while it isn't listening on the board is told to start, so you can work on the page with it straight away.

Select blocks on a page (drag a box, ⇧-click, or click a group) and they become the context of the page's chat bar: a chip there says "2 blocks", and its ✕ lets go of them. What you type then starts a thread about those blocks, with your message as its first, and its answer peeks over the bar, as one asked over the page does: it goes to the page's agent (or the one you `@`-mention), through the General lens unless the message opens with `^Lens`. The agent talks it through with you there first, in plain words with its reasons, and makes nothing until you ask for it or agree to what it suggests. Say what to make and it makes it straight away, in the thread. Blocks it adds for the thread are shown in a group made beside the blocks, titled from your message, with a *from a thread* chip that opens the thread. The group is made with the first thing the agent makes, not when you ask, so a thread that is only talk leaves the page as it was. The thread keeps the blocks it was about as you selected them, and goes on if you delete one later. Each message sent with blocks selected starts a thread of its own; to carry on in it, open it from Home's conversation, where it is tagged *Asked on* the page *· 2 blocks*, or find it with search. An agent works it with `reply_in_thread`, passing the thread's id to `add_block`. A request about blocks asked before this, when what you sent left the agent to choose what to make, is answered the old way, in a group made beside the blocks: `take_request`, then blocks in the group, then `finish_request` with its note; until an agent takes it, the group's header can cancel it.

**Without its Tricot tools.** When the agent you ask in its own terminal has no Tricot tools there (Tricot wasn't running when it started, or it isn't connected), it runs [`tricot show`](../cli.md#showing-something-from-an-agents-terminal) in its shell instead: that starts Tricot if needed, makes the page on the board used in that folder as the agent, a block for each file, and hands it the link to give you. When the folder doesn't say which board, the agent asks you, and a new board is made only when you say so. What you then ask it on the page, from the chat bar or about blocks you select, wakes it through `tricot show --listen`, and it answers there with `tricot show --reply`.

A canvas is a whiteboard of typed blocks, so both sides can read it: you see and edit the visual, agents read and write the structure. What you sketch on it is typed too: shapes and labels are blocks agents read and draw, and a hand drawing is a block they read.

| Block | You | Agents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Note | Markdown, double-click to edit. Paste text, or drop selected text from another app, to make one of it. A task list (`- [ ] step`) ticks in place, and the header says how far along it is (3 of 7 done) | `text`; `items` replaces its task list, keeping ticks where the words match. A `checklist` with `items` is still accepted and makes a note |
| Table | Double-click a cell to edit; add or remove rows and columns | `columns`, `rows` |
| Diagram | Rendered Mermaid, double-click to edit the source | `source` |
| Image | Paste a screenshot, drop a file (or an image dragged from another page, when the browser hands over the file; otherwise its address lands as a link), or use *Image* | `image_path` or `image_base64`; returned as images |
| Link | A card with the site's icon, title and host; click the title to open it. Tricot draws the icons of the sites people link most (GitHub, Figma, Google Docs, Notion, Jira and others) and a plain link icon for the rest, so nothing is fetched from the site for its card. Paste a web address, drop a link from the address bar or a page, or use *Link*; double-click to edit | `url` (http or https) and an optional `title`; listed as `link "Title" → url` |
| Website | A site whose files are in a folder on this computer, or a local dev server (`localhost`), shown live on its card. *Open* takes it to the whole canvas: *Browse* to click around it at a desktop's or a phone's width, *Edit* to pick any part of a page and leave a note on it or ask an agent to change it; the thread opens on the element, and the page reloads in place when its files change. It is served from a local address of its own, never Tricot's, so the site's scripts can't reach the board. Only when Tricot runs on this computer alone; a public site is a link | `folder` (inside your home folder) or `url` (`http://localhost:PORT/`), optional `page`; requests on its elements come with the page, the element and, for a folder, its file and line |
| Shape and drawing | Drawn with the tools in their own capsule on the chat bar's left, there while a canvas you can edit is shown. *Shape* holds a rectangle, an ellipse, a diamond and an arrow: a click puts it on with the last one you picked, and the tray over the tools then offers all four first, beside the colour and the line, so one place picks the shape and how it looks (R, O, D and A pick one too). Drag on the canvas to draw a shape the size you drag (⇧ for a square or a circle), or click for one of its own size, and type its words; drag from one block to another with the arrow to join them, the block it will join ringed as you drag over it, or anywhere else for a free arrow from where you pressed to where you let go (⇧ holds it to 45°), which you move by its line, resize, colour and, with a double-click, write on. *Text* (T) places a label where you click. *Pen* (P) draws by hand, the strokes you draw close together kept as one drawing. V or Esc goes back to selecting. While a shape, the arrow, Text or the Pen is on, a tray over the tools holds its options, used for what you draw next: a colour, and a shape's line (thin, medium or thick), a label's size (S, M or L) and bold, or the pen's width; the arrow has its line alone. Drawings are read, as a hand drawing of so many strokes, and only people make them |

Layout meaning is explicit so agents don't have to infer it: **groups** have names, and **links** between blocks have labels (drag from a block's right edge).

**Copy and paste between canvases.** Select blocks (a group takes its blocks along) and press ⌘C (Ctrl C elsewhere), then paste with ⌘V on any canvas of this Tricot, on this board or another. The copies are new blocks centred on what you're looking at, keeping how they sit to one another and the links between them. A copy brings what the block shows now: no pins, comments or earlier versions. A link to a Google Doc, Figma file, GitHub, Linear or Slack copies as the same link, and an image shows the same file as the original. Anywhere else, what you copied is the blocks' words as plain text; and if the blocks have been deleted since, you're told, and the words paste as a note. It stays on this computer's Tricot: nothing is carried to another one.

Your edits reach agents as specific updates: *checked "Audit call sites"*, *set "status" in row 3 to "done"*, *moved table "Audit" into group "Evidence"*. Plain moves and resizes sync to other viewers without being logged.

**Pins.** On any canvas you can pin a precise part of a block: select words in a note (a task in its task list included), click a table cell (drag for a range, click a column name for the column, or widen cells to their rows or columns in the prompt), drag a box on an image (it's numbered on the picture), click a box or an arrow in a diagram (a flowchart's nodes and arrows, a sequence diagram's participants and messages, a class diagram's classes and relations, a state diagram's states and transitions, or an entity relationship diagram's entities and relationships, and in any other kind its labelled pieces: a mindmap's nodes, a Gantt chart's tasks, a pie chart's slices), or use *Pin* on a selected link card. The part turns amber and a prompt quotes it. *Comment* starts a thread on the pin, which the part's 💬 chip opens and counts the comments of. Agents reply there; *Resolve* closes the thread and quiets its chip, and a reply reopens it. The part stays blue. Pins follow their part through edits (words by the text around them, cells by their column names and contents, boxes as fractions of the picture, a diagram's boxes by their id in the Mermaid source, its arrows by the two they join, another kind's pieces by their words); a part edited away shows as changed (*text changed*, *node removed*) and stays listed. Hover a chip and click its ✕ to unpin it: its thread goes with its pin. Agents see pins and open threads under their block in `get_canvas`, and reply with `comment`.
